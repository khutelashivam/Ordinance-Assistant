# Grading System

## 17.1 Awarding of Grades

At the end of a semester or Summer School, the concerned Course Instructor awards a letter grade for each course.

The instructor considers the student's performance in:

- Quizzes
- Assignments
- Mid-semester examination
- End-semester examination
- Viva
- Laboratory work
- Attendance and regularity in classes

Grades are submitted by the Course Instructor through a signed grade sheet in the prescribed format.

The grade sheet is submitted to the office of the Head of Department within the prescribed time after the End-Semester or Supplementary Examination.

## Letter Grades and Grade Points

The Institute uses the following letter grades:

| Letter Grade | Academic Performance | Grade Point |
|---|---|---:|
| O | Outstanding | 10 |
| A | Excellent | 9 |
| B | Very Good | 8 |
| C | Good | 7 |
| D | Average | 6 |
| P | Below Average | 5 |
| F | Fail | 0 |
| I | Incomplete | — |
| X | Debarred | — |

The grades **I** and **X** are transitional grades representing:

- **I:** Incomplete
- **X:** Debarred

## 17.2 Relative Grading System

When a course is taught by more than one teacher for different batches, a committee consisting of:

- Course Coordinator — Chairperson
- Other faculty members teaching the course — Members

will finalize the grades.

If necessary, marks from different batches may be normalized before final grades are awarded.

### Minimum Requirement for Passing

No student who has obtained **35% or more marks** should be awarded the failing grade **F**.

The Chairman, Senate may constitute a committee chaired by the Dean/Associate Dean of Academic Affairs to examine grades awarded in a course if required.

### Normal Distribution Guideline

The ordinance provides a relative grading guideline based on:

- Mean marks: X̄
- Standard deviation: s

The guideline provides approximate distributions for grades O, A, B, C, D, P and F.

## 17.3 Absence from End-Semester Examination

A student who does not appear in the End-Semester Examination for any reason is awarded an:

**F grade**

This applies irrespective of performance in the Mid-Semester Examination and Sessional Awards.

## 17.4 Successful Completion of a Course

A student successfully completes a subject and earns its credits when the student receives a letter grade other than:

- I
- X
- F

An **F grade** in any subject means failure in that subject.

## 17.5 Transitional Grades

### Grade I — Incomplete

A teacher may award an **I grade** when a student was compelled to miss the End-Semester Examination because of:

- Illness
- Accident that prevented the student from appearing in the examination
- Natural disaster or calamity during the End-Semester Examination that, in the opinion of the Institute, required the student to leave the campus

The student is eligible for an I grade only when:

- Attendance is complete and satisfactory.
- Performance in other assessment components is complete and satisfactory.

### Grade X — Debarred

A student may receive an **X grade** after being debarred from an End-Semester Examination due to:

1. Unsatisfactory attendance.
2. Disciplinary action by the Institute.
3. Recommendation of the Unfair Means Committee for malpractice in an examination.

If debarred because of attendance, attendance below **50%** results in debarment from the End-Semester Examination in that course.

A student awarded an X grade may re-register for the relevant subject or semester after the debarment period expires, provided that other provisions of the regulations do not prevent the student from doing so.

### Conversion of I Grade

An I grade is converted to a letter grade after successful clearance in a Supplementary Examination conducted in July each year.
